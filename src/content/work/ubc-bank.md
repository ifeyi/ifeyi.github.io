## The brief

UBC is a Cameroonian bank. The brief was to design a concept corporate website that would let visitors quickly find a branch or ATM near them, browse the bank's products, and contact the institution — with a visual identity that signaled stability and clarity rather than the over-decorated style common in regional banking sites.

The work was a design study rather than a production engagement. That gave it room to be an honest exercise in *what would a Cameroonian bank's website look like if it took its visitors seriously?*

## What made it hard

Banking websites in the region have a specific kind of visual entropy: too many call-out boxes, gradient buttons, marketing copy stacked next to product copy stacked next to disclosures. The reason is institutional — every department gets a tile on the homepage — but the result is that finding a nearby ATM is harder than it should be.

The brief was to undo that entropy. Design constraint: privilege the practical tasks that bring people to a bank's site (find a branch, find an ATM, check a product, get in touch) over the marketing requirement to display every product line above the fold.

## Approach

**Tasks, not departments, on the homepage.** The hero of the homepage is a clear pathway into the things visitors actually come for. Product browse is one click away, not the visual centerpiece. The bank's marketing identity — its name, its claim, its photograph — has space, but does not crowd the practical paths.

**Branch and ATM finder as a first-class screen.** Most bank sites bury the branch finder under "Contact" or "About". I designed it as a primary navigation item with its own dedicated screen — map plus list, filterable by city and by service offered, on both desktop and mobile.

**A restrained design system.** A single primary color, a calm neutral palette, generous whitespace, one display weight and one body weight in the typography. The system holds together because every component answers to it, not because the brand is loud.

**Visual hierarchy that respects literacy diversity.** Many bank visitors in the region are not the sort of visitor a Northern-European bank's UX assumes. The visual hierarchy was tested for whether the most important calls to action read clearly to someone scanning quickly, on mobile, with imperfect reading conditions.

## What it produced

A homepage, branch-finder screen, and product-page concept, plus a small design system covering type, color, components, and key states. The work informs how I think about the *interface layer* of banking systems — the part that the architecture work in the rest of this portfolio is ultimately serving.

## Reflection

Banking architecture and banking interface design are usually treated as separate disciplines, with the result that institutions end up with a competent backend and a confusing frontend, or vice versa. The most interesting work happens when both layers know each other well. Designing UBC alongside doing architecture work has made me a better architect — I am more attentive to which design decisions at the interface depend on backend decisions further down, and which architecture decisions silently constrain what is possible at the interface.
