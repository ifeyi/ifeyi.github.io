## The brief

Soutenable is a nonprofit advocating for social equality and ecology. They needed a web presence that explained their mission, hosted the project work and the writing they wanted to publish, and — importantly — did not contradict its own message by being assembled from tools and templates that depended on exploitation, surveillance, or unpaid creative labor.

That last requirement is unusual in a web project. Most briefs treat tooling as invisible infrastructure. Soutenable explicitly did not.

## What made it hard

Two things, in tension.

**Editorial quality.** The site has to feel like the work of a serious organization. Generic-template energy would undermine the mission, which is a serious one. The visual language had to do real work.

**Material discipline.** The standard ways to get to editorial quality — premium stock photography, enterprise CMS platforms, third-party tracking and analytics, AI-generated illustration — each carry trade-offs Soutenable was not willing to make. Stock photography is rarely consented; enterprise CMS pricing depends on capturing institutional dependency; tracking turns visitors into resources; AI illustration in 2025 sits on top of unsettled questions about who gets paid for the styles and labor it is sampling.

Designing within those constraints is harder than designing without them. It is also a more honest articulation of what the nonprofit was advocating for. A website that says one thing in copy and another in its supply chain is not credible.

## Approach

**Hand-drawn illustration.** The site is illustrated by hand, by people who were paid for the work. The illustrations carry the visual identity in place of stock photography. The aesthetic is consistent because the maker is consistent — not because a templated style has been imposed.

**Lightweight stack.** The site uses a static-site build with no tracking, no third-party analytics, and no JavaScript-heavy frameworks. Pages load fast on slow connections — a real concern for the audience the organization works with.

**Editorial layout.** The pages prioritize reading. The mission page, the "who we are" page, the writing — all use a single-column reading layout with generous typographic hierarchy rather than a marketing-grid template. The illustrations break the column at a measured cadence, the way a good magazine alternates art and text.

**Mobile as the primary surface.** Most visitors come on mobile. The mobile layout was the design starting point, with the desktop layout developed as a derivative. This is the opposite of the usual "design desktop, then make it work on mobile" workflow — and it produces different decisions about which elements to emphasize.

## What it produced

A live site that hosts the organization's mission, the team's identity, and a growing body of writing. The design system is small enough that the team can extend it themselves without a designer in the loop — pages can be added without breaking the visual coherence.

## Reflection

Designing under self-imposed constraints is generative. The Soutenable site is not the most visually elaborate piece of work I have done. It is the one where the constraints made the design *more* coherent rather than less, because every decision had to justify itself against the brief.

There is a version of this argument that applies to architecture work too. Every system inherits the values of the tools it is built from. Soutenable made that inheritance explicit and refused some of it. That posture — *the supply chain of the design is part of the design* — is one I want to carry into other work.
